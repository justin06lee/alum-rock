No. 01Registration

Registration & renewals

New registrations and renewals, including ones that have expired. Renew and print your sticker in one stop.

On M&P's list

  • Car Registration
  • Renewal Registration
  • New Registration
  • Renew registration & print sticker
  • Reinstate Registration
  • Replace Registration
  • Submit Proof of Insurance

What to bring

  • Your renewal notice, or your plate number and the last 5 digits of the VIN
  • A passing smog check, if your notice asks for one. The station sends it to the DMV electronically
  • Proof of insurance, if your notice asks for it
  • Payment for the DMV fees, any late penalties and the service fee

No. 02Buying or selling

Title transfers

The buyer has 10 days from the sale to report it to the DMV; the seller has 5 days to file a release of liability.

On M&P's list

  • Title Transfer
  • Clean Titles Transfer
  • Registered/Legal Owner Transfers
  • Notice of Transfer & Release of Liability (NRL)
  • Lien Release

What to bring

  • The California title (pink slip), signed by the seller and the buyer
  • The odometer reading, on the title or a REG 262. Most newer vehicles need one
  • A smog certificate from the seller, unless the car is four model years old or newer, or it's a family transfer
  • A bill of sale (REG 135) showing the price
  • The buyer's ID and proof of insurance

No. 03New to California

Out-of-state transfers

Register a car from another state, a brand-new vehicle with a manufacturer's certificate, or one that isn't in the DMV's system yet. Moving here? Start with this.

On M&P's list

  • Out of State Transfer
  • Vehicles with Manufacturer Statement of Origin (MSO) or Out-of-State Title
  • Vehicles not in DMV System

What to bring

  • Your out-of-state title, or if the car is financed, your lender's name and address and your account number
  • Your most recent out-of-state registration
  • A California smog certificate (gas 1976 and newer; diesel 1998 and newer under 14,001 lbs GVWR)
  • The car itself, for the VIN verification (REG 31)
  • Proof of California insurance and your ID

No. 04REG 31

VIN verification

The vehicle identification number is checked against your paperwork and recorded on the DMV's REG 31 form. Needed for out-of-state cars and some other transactions.

On M&P's list

  • Vin Verification
  • Vehicle Verification (REG 31)

What to bring

  • The vehicle. It has to be looked at in person
  • The title, registration or bill of sale it's being checked against
  • Your ID

No. 05REG 227

Duplicate & replacement titles

Lost, stolen or damaged pink slip, including when you need to sell a car whose title you can't find.

On M&P's list

  • Duplicate Titles

What to bring

  • If a lender is still listed: their signature on the REG 227, notarized, or a notarized lien release (REG 166)

New titles are printed and mailed by the DMV.

Moving here

New to California? You have 20 days.

Once you're a California resident (taking a job here or renting a home counts), the state gives you 20 days to register your car. Out-of-state transfers and VIN verification are both on M&P's service list. The usual order:

  1. Get a California smog check at a licensed smog station. The result goes to the DMV electronically.
  2. Find your out-of-state title and registration. If the car is financed, have your lender's name, address and your account number.
  3. Bring the car for the VIN verification (REG 31). The vehicle has to be inspected in person.
  4. Register it at M&P. Call (408) 391-1549 first to check what your vehicle needs.

Missing something? The DMV's advice is to submit your application and fees on time anyway, even without every document, so you avoid penalties.

Bought it new in another state? A car that's under two years old, had fewer than 7,500 miles when you bought it and isn't certified to California emissions standards usually can't be registered here. Call before you go.

Questions

Good to know before you go

Is M&P the DMV?

No. M&P Car Registration Services is a private business that the California DMV lists as a DMV Partner, which the DMV describes as “Businesses authorized by the DMV to handle certain registration services, often with much shorter wait times (if any!). Additional fees may be applied by this partner.”

Driver licenses, ID cards and REAL ID are handled only at DMV field offices.

Do I need a smog check?

If you're renewing, your renewal notice will say so. Most cars more than eight model years old need one every two years; newer ones pay a smog abatement fee instead.

When a car is sold, the seller provides the smog certificate, unless the car is four model years old or newer (the buyer pays a smog transfer fee instead) or the sale is between family: parents, grandparents, children, grandchildren, siblings, spouses and domestic partners. Cars coming in from another state need one to register here.

Electric cars, motorcycles and gas cars from 1975 and older are exempt. Smog checks are done at licensed smog stations, which send the result to the DMV electronically.

My registration is expired. What now?

Renew it as soon as you can. California has no grace period: DMV late penalties start the day after it expires and grow the longer it goes.

Try not to drive it until it's sorted. You can be ticketed, and a car more than six months expired can be towed. If a failed smog check is what's holding you up, ask M&P about a temporary operating permit.

Can someone else go in for me?

For a renewal, someone else can usually bring your renewal notice (or your plate number and the last 5 digits of the VIN) and pay. For a transfer or a duplicate title, they bring the paperwork already signed by the right people.

If an owner can't sign in person, a power of attorney on the DMV's REG 260 form lets someone sign on their behalf.

Do they handle salvage titles, export and Copart?

Yes, all three are on M&P's own service list, along with reviving junk and salvage vehicles. What you'll need depends on the vehicle's history, so call (408) 391-1549 with your paperwork in hand.

When will my title arrive?

California titles are printed and mailed by the DMV, not by the registration service, so they come by mail after your transaction, usually within a few weeks.
