Why it rubs

The tire hits the lip inside the fender.

Most fenders have a lip folded under the edge. With the factory wheels there's room to spare. Change the setup and the tire can reach that lip over bumps, in turns or with passengers in the back, and you hear it, or it cuts the tire.

  • Wider wheels & tiresMore tire sitting further out.
  • Spacers or a lower offsetPushes the wheel toward the fender edge.
  • LoweringLess space between the tire and the arch.
  • A bigger brake kitOften needs spacers to clear, and the rub starts there.

Fender work

Rolled, pulled, flared or shaved.

Cars, trucks and old-schools. Every setup is different, so there's no price list: text Aaron a photo of the wheel and fender and he'll quote it.

  1. Fender rolling

    The inner lip is folded up flat so the tire clears it. Front, rear or all four.

  2. Fender pulling

    The arch is pushed outward a little, for setups where rolling alone isn't enough.

  3. Fender flaring

    More room again, for wide wheel and tire setups.

  4. Shaved fenders

    Trimming the lip, and the plastic liner if it's in the way.

  5. Quarter-panel rolls

    The rear arches on cars where the rear fender is part of the body.

  6. Wheel fitment

    Getting a new wheel and tire setup to sit right on the car.
